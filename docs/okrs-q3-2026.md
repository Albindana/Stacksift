# Quarterly OKRs — Q3 2026 (July – September 2026)

> **M6.5 deliverable** — OKRs formulated with AI assistance (Claude Sonnet 4.6, full codebase context)
> then critically reviewed and refined by the product team.
>
> Grounding sources: `docs/product-metrics.md` (NSM + GA4 events), `docs/slo.md`,
> `src/backend/StackSift.Domain/PlanLimits.cs`, `docs/architecture.md`, recent git history.

---

## Methodology

OKRs were drafted by feeding Claude the live codebase, the North Star Metric definition, the GA4
event taxonomy, SLO targets, plan limits, and recent commits into a single prompt. The model
produced a first draft; the team then critically reviewed each KR against two gates:

1. **Measurability** — can we instrument this with existing GA4 events + Prometheus + server-side
   data, or do we need new telemetry?
2. **Causality** — does improving this KR actually move the NSM, or is it a vanity proxy?

KRs that failed either gate were revised or flagged (see Critical Review below).

## Objective 1 — Establish a repeatable AI triage habit in our beta cohort

> **Why this objective.** StackSift's NSM is *Weekly Active Organisations that triage ≥1 incident
> with AI*. An org that triggers AI analysis once is activated; an org that does it every week has
> formed the habit that drives retention and word-of-mouth. This objective directly attacks the NSM
> from two angles — breadth (more orgs) and depth (more analyses per org).

| # | Key Result | Measurement | Baseline | Target |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------|--------|
| KR1.1 | Weekly Active Organisations (WAO) triggering ≥1 `ai_analysis_triggered` event per rolling 7-day window | Server-side count from `AiAnalysis` table + GA4 `ai_analysis_triggered` event | TBD — establish week 1 of Q3 | **≥20 WAO by 2026-09-30** |
| KR1.2 | Median AI analyses per active org per week | Server-side aggregate: `COUNT(ai_analysis_triggered) / active_org_count` rolling 7 days | TBD | **≥5 analyses / org / week** |
| KR1.3 | 4-week retention for orgs that complete their first AI triage session | Cohort: orgs whose first `ai_analysis_triggered` fires in July; check if they trigger again in weeks 2, 3, and 4 | Not yet tracked | **≥60% return in ≥2 of 4 subsequent weeks** |

## Objective 2 — Remove activation friction so new orgs reach their first AI insight the same day they sign up

> **Why this objective.** The AARRR funnel has a known cliff between `log_source_created` and
> `ai_analysis_triggered`. A user who signs up, sets up a log source, and never gets to AI triage
> has not experienced the product's core value. This objective shrinks the valley between those two
> events.

| # | Key Result | Measurement | Baseline | Target |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------|--------|
| KR2.1 | Median time from `sign_up` → `ai_analysis_triggered` | Server-side: `MIN(ai_analysis.CreatedAt) - org.CreatedAt` per organisation | TBD | **≤24 hours** |
| KR2.2 | `sign_up` → `log_source_created` funnel conversion rate | GA4 Funnel Exploration (events: `sign_up` → `log_source_created`) | TBD | **≥70%** |
| KR2.3 | `log_source_created` → `ai_analysis_triggered` funnel conversion rate | GA4 Funnel Exploration (events: `log_source_created` → `ai_analysis_triggered`) | TBD | **≥50%** |

## Objective 3 — Prove willingness to pay before Series A conversations

> **Why this objective.** The Stripe billing system is live (Free / Indie / Team plans). Paying
> customers are the clearest signal of product-market fit. This objective converts the payment
> infrastructure into actual revenue signals by the end of Q3.

| # | Key Result | Measurement | Baseline | Target |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------|--------|
| KR3.1 | % of activated orgs (≥3 `ai_analysis_triggered` events) that upgrade to Indie or Team | Server-side: `upgraded_orgs / activated_orgs * 100` | TBD | **≥20%** |

| KR3.3 | Monthly subscription churn rate among paying orgs | Server-side: `cancellations_this_month / paying_orgs_at_start_of_month * 100` (only tracked once sample ≥5) | N/A | **≤5%** |

## Critical Review (AI self-critique + team assessment)

This section documents where each OKR is strong, where it has measurement gaps, and what action is
needed to make it valid. Being honest here is more useful than a clean-looking OKR deck.

### What the AI draft got right

- All three objectives connect causally to the NSM and to each other: activation (O2) feeds habit
  (O1) feeds revenue (O3). No objective is a vanity metric island.
- KR3.2 (10 paying orgs) is the best-defined KR in the set — server-side from Stripe data,
  unambiguous, and the most honest signal of product-market fit.
- Using the GA4 AARRR funnel events already implemented in `src/app/lib/analytics.ts` means KR2.2
  and KR2.3 can be measured immediately without new instrumentation.

### Instrumentation gaps to fix before Q3 begins

| KR | Gap | Fix |
|----|-----|-----|
| KR1.3 (4-week retention) | `ai_analysis_triggered` fires `incident_id` but not `org_id`. Cohort analysis requires server-side join. GA4 alone cannot compute org-level cohort retention. | Add `org_id` as a GA4 user property in `trackEvent` (set on login in `useAuthStore`), OR compute cohort retention purely server-side in a Hangfire weekly report job. |
| KR2.1 (median TTF ≤24h) | `first_log_ingested` fires client-side only when the dashboard is open during the 0→>0 transition. A user who ingests via API without opening the dashboard never fires the event. | Replace GA4 measurement of this KR with server-side `MIN(LogEntry.CreatedAt) - Organization.CreatedAt` query in the Hangfire reconciliation job — ground truth is already in PostgreSQL. |
| KR1.1 baseline (WAO) | WAO count is not yet computed anywhere. Without a week-1 baseline, the target (≥20) is unvalidated. If current WAO is 2, ≥20 is 10x growth — likely too aggressive. If current WAO is 15, ≥20 is modest. | Instrument a weekly Hangfire job or Grafana panel that counts distinct `OrgId` values in `AiAnalysis` with `CreatedAt` in the last 7 days. Run for 2 weeks before committing to the ≥20 target. |

### KRs that are aspirational without a growth plan

- **KR1.1 (≥20 WAO)** assumes we have or can acquire 20+ active engineering teams in one quarter.
  This is only achievable with a structured beta outreach or referral program. If no such program
  exists, the KR should be reduced to ≥10 WAO with a clear "how": direct outreach to 5 companies +
  1 Product Hunt launch.
- **KR3.2 (10 paying orgs)** is ambitious for a product in closed beta. A $19/month Indie plan
  has a short sales cycle; the risk is not conversion rate but *top of funnel volume*. Ensure the
  waitlist or beta invite pipeline can generate ≥50 qualified sign-ups in Q3.

### KR3.3 sample-size caveat

Monthly churn on a base of 10 orgs means a single cancellation = 10% churn — trivially breaching
the ≤5% target. KR3.3 should only be evaluated once ≥5 paying orgs have been active for ≥30 days
continuously. Until that threshold, track churn as an informational metric, not a scored KR.

### What was cut from the AI draft

The AI initially proposed a reliability objective (API availability ≥99.5%, P95 latency ≤800ms).
These are valid operational goals but belong in `docs/slo.md` as standing SLO commitments, not
quarterly OKRs — they don't differentiate a good quarter from a bad one for a growth-stage product.
They were moved to the existing SLO document rather than inflating the OKR count.

## OKR Scoring rubric (end of Q3)

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 0.0–0.3 | Failed — we didn't try hard enough or the target was wrong |
| 0.4–0.6 | Made progress — partial success, learnings documented |
| 0.7–1.0 | Success — green (1.0 means we set it too easy; ideal is 0.7–0.8) |

Score each KR independently at the Q3 retrospective. An OKR that scores 1.0 every quarter was
probably never ambitious enough.
